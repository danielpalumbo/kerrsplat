# The large-N basis (2026-09-10 evening, onward)

## Direction

Daniel, 2026-09-10: fixed-N parcel fits have low scientific value, and no fit to anything like
the 2017 EHT data should claim a well-constrained four-dimensional plasma. The program is the
opposite of the tomography of Levis, Feng and Bouman, who needed restrictive assumptions to
get unique fits from poor data: move to the large-N limit of the splat basis as the 3D vision
community uses it, an over-complete set of parcels with hygiene and sparsity rather than a
hypothesis about their number, fit slow-light movies of I, Q, U and V that carry the n = 1
and n = 2 sub-images, keep the assumptions weak, and show that the plasma comes out nearly
unique when the data are rich enough, then map where uniqueness breaks as the data are taken
away (V, then Q and U, then frequencies, then n = 2, then frames). Every statement is about
fields on a spacetime grid, the emissivity, the density-weighted temperature, field and
velocity, never about parcel parameters, because the basis has invariances that are not
physics (permutations, splits of one parcel into two, the density–temperature–field trade at
one frequency).

The Sgr A* 2017 work of the same day (`2026-09-10_sgra_timeresolved.md`) stands as the
validation of the VLBI and instrument pipeline on real data, nothing more.

## Where the cost was

Both passes of the dual sweep, and the forward transport, looped over every parcel at every
sample of every ray, with a cheap bounding-sphere test (`outside_support`) before the
coefficients. On the CPU backend at 16² pixels and 40 samples the forward image and the
gradient scaled linearly with N from the first parcel, 0.006 s and 0.010 s at N = 8 to 0.30 s
and 0.51 s at N = 512, and the per-ray gradient buffer of the dual sweep, npix × 21 × N, was
22 MB there and would be 2.7 GB at 128² pixels and a thousand parcels.

## Per-ray parcel lists

`ray_lists(cache, params, t_obs; nmax, slab)` builds, once per frame, the list of parcels
whose bounding sphere one of a ray's samples enters (the samples beyond the half-orbit cutoff
excluded, as in the transport), in two launches: a count, then the fill into
`capacity = maximum(count)` slots, so that no ray overflows. A `PolarizedSplats` carrying the
lists presents ray `j` with a `RaySubset` through `Transfer.ray_model`, the one hook the
transport consumer and the two dual-sweep kernels call, and every sum over parcels then runs
over the ray's list. The sums are the same to the bit, because a parcel outside its support
contributes exactly zero. The per-ray gradient sums go into the slots of the ray's list
(npix × 21 × capacity) and are gathered parcel by parcel in ascending ray order through a
CSR built on the host, so the result stays independent of the order of the threads. The
culling is on by default above sixteen parcels in `polarized_gradient!`, `sweep_passes`,
`chi2_gradient!` and `image_loss_gradient!` (`cull`), and the gate `test_ray_lists` checks
the lists' structure, the identity of the image and the gradient with the dense path, with
and without the truncation.

## What the lists buy, and when

On the GPU (64² pixels, 60 samples, parcels of scales 0.15 and 0.6 M scattered over 3–8 M):

| parcels | list capacity / mean length | lists | tails, lists / dense | gradient, lists / dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 26 / 8.9 | 0.008 s | 0.13 / 0.11 s | 0.59 / 0.49 s |
| 128 | 96 / 34 | 0.027 s | 0.47 / 0.38 s | 2.1 / 1.6 s |
| 512 | 374 / 142 | 0.11 s | 2.1 / 1.6 s | 8.8 / 6.4 s |
| 2048 | 1488 / 567 | 0.47 s | 8.2 s | 36 s |

The lists are exact (the image identical, the gradient to 1e-15) and useless at this parcel
density: a ray's list holds 28% of the parcels whatever their number, and the list path runs
30% slower per element than the dense one (an indirect index per element). The reason is
the support test itself. The bounding sphere is the largest scale times 7.4σ (the weight
cutoff of 1e-12), so a 0.6 M parcel claims a sphere of 4.4 M radius, and a ray through a
shell of 5 M radius passes within 4.4 M of most of the parcels on it. The dense path already
skips those with the same cheap test, and the expensive work, the coefficients of the
parcels whose weight is above the cutoff at a sample, is the same in both. The lists pay
only in the regime they are meant for, many small parcels, where the sphere test dominates
the dense pass: for 0.2 M parcels the sphere is 1.5 M and a ray's list is a few per cent of
thousands. That is the next benchmark (`--size 0.2`).

With every parcel at 0.2 M (the same screen and samples):

| parcels | list capacity / mean length | lists | tails, lists / dense | gradient, lists / dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 47 / 6.0 | 0.03 s | 0.08 / 0.13 s | 0.41 / 0.41 s |
| 512 | 176 / 24 | 0.11 s | 0.36 / 0.50 s | 1.5 / 1.4 s |
| 2048 | 648 / 97 | 0.48 s | 1.4 / 2.0 s | 5.5 / 5.3 s |
| 8192 | 2656 / 391 | 1.9 s | 5.6 s | 22 s |

Now the lists cull 95% of the parcels, and the time still barely moves: the tails 1.5×
faster, the gradient not at all, both still linear in N. So the cost is not the sphere tests
the lists remove; it is what happens to the parcels that pass them, and with a 7.4σ sphere
most of those pass with weights between 1e-12 and 1e-3 and get the full treatment: the
13-partial dual weight, the 7-partial dual synchrotron coefficients, the local frame, for a
contribution the data cannot see. The lever is the weight cutoff itself, 1e-12 at present:
at 1e-6 the sphere shrinks to 5.3σ and the volume a parcel claims to 35%; the total's
discontinuity at the boundary is the cutoff times one pair's share of the total, of order
1e-11, far below the finite-difference gates at 1e-5.

At a cutoff of 1e-6 (the mixed 0.15 and 0.6 M parcels of the first table; a small probe ran
on the card at the same time, so the times are slightly pessimistic):

| parcels | mean list length, 1e-12 → 1e-6 | gradient, lists / dense, 1e-12 → 1e-6 |
|---|---|---|
| 512 | 142 → 77 | 8.8 / 6.4 s → 5.4 / 3.9 s |
| 2048 | 567 → 311 | 36 s → 20 s |
| 8192 | | 70 s (lists) |

1.7× across the board, the image and the gradient still identical between the two paths, and
the cutoff is now 1e-6 in the package. Its effect on a fit: the two-parcel movie fit of
`test_fit` run at both cutoffs with the same seed ends at χ² 1366.19 and 1366.77 after a
hundred iterations, a relative difference of 4e-4 in the χ² of a fit that stops far from
converged, and every derivative gate (the dual and Enzyme sweeps against each other, the
half-orbit truncation, the spacetime duals against finite differences at 1e-5) passes
unchanged. The lists remain a wash on this card at these sizes;
they will matter when the parcels are small and numerous enough that the sphere tests
dominate, which is the regime the hygiene will produce, and they cost nothing to keep on.
The per-element overhead of the list path (30%) and the underfilled card (64² pixels is 4096
threads against 3072 cores, with heavy per-thread state) are the next two things to take
from the kernels: batching frames into one launch fills the card.

With the cutoff at 1e-6 and every parcel at 0.2 M, the lists do pay:

| parcels | mean list length | tails, lists / dense | gradient, lists / dense | gradient at 1e-12, lists |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 11.6 | 0.15 / 0.36 s | 0.68 / 1.07 s | 1.5 s |
| 2048 | 47 | 0.57 / 1.42 s | 2.4 / 3.9 s | 5.5 s |
| 8192 | 190 | 2.3 s | 10.3 s | 22 s |

The tails run 2.5× faster over the lists than dense and the gradient 1.6×, and against the
starting point (dense, 1e-12) the gradient at 2048 parcels is 2.3× faster, at 8192 parcels
2.2×. The gradient remains linear in N at about 1.2 ms per parcel per frame at 64² × 60 on
this card, which is the cost of the parcels a ray does see: a thousand-parcel movie of four
frames and two frequencies is ten seconds per iteration here and, at the FP64 ratio, a
fraction of a second on an H200.
